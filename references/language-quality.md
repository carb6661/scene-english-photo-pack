# Language quality gate

Apply this gate to every candidate word, phrase, and model sentence.

## Selection priorities

1. Scene relevance: the learner should be able to connect the item to something visible or to the scene's normal function.
2. Collocational naturalness: prefer chunks native speakers actually combine.
3. Reusability: prefer language transferable to common education, health, work, technology, environment, transport, shopping, community, and lifestyle topics.
4. Productive value: balance words the learner can recognize with chunks they can immediately say or write.
5. Level fit: mostly B1-B2 for Cards 1-3 and B2-C1 for Cards 4-6. Do not inflate difficulty for appearance.

## Register

- Mark conversational items as `[口语]` and formal academic frames as `[写作]` when confusion is possible.
- Do not use stiff academic phrasing in a personal speaking answer.
- Do not use slang, vague fillers, or contractions in a formal Task 2 sentence unless illustrating a contrast in register.

## Common repairs

Repair or reject source phrases that are grammatical but unnatural, dated, too broad, or semantically wrong. Frequent examples:

- Prefer `gain / develop communication skills` to `grasp communication skills`.
- Prefer `make a contribution to society` to `make contribution to society`.
- Prefer `the widespread use of the internet` to `the proliferation of Internet` in ordinary learner writing.
- Prefer `adapt to technological change` to vague frames such as `adapt oneself to the development of ...`.
- Prefer a precise verb (`reduce`, `ease`, `address`, `discourage`) to generic `give a big push to`.
- Avoid clichés such as `with the development of society`, `every coin has two sides`, and repeated `more and more`.

## IELTS-oriented checks

- Reward precision and control, not memorized complexity.
- Keep claims defensible: use `can`, `may`, `tends to`, or `is likely to` when causality is not certain.
- Use cohesive links only when the logical relation is real.
- Vary grammar through relative clauses, participle clauses, concession, and controlled complex sentences; do not force all of them into one paragraph.
- For Speaking, use specific detail, natural sequencing, and a personal or social angle rather than a miniature essay.

## Pronunciation

- Default to British IPA because the learner is preparing for IELTS.
- Preserve standard UK/US lexical distinctions when relevant, for example `plaster (UK) / adhesive bandage (US)`.
- IPA and spelling must match the headword. For plural phrases, transcribe the spoken plural if it is the taught form.

## Final audit

Before rendering, confirm:

- no duplicated item without a pedagogical reason;
- no unsupported object or action presented as visibly certain;
- no Chinese translation that changes the English claim;
- no fake quotation, citation, band guarantee, or dictionary attribution;
- no phrase copied wholesale from a copyrighted source beyond a short ordinary expression;
- examples use correct articles, number, prepositions, and punctuation.
